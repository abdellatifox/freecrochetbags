This **crochet market tote bag pattern** gives you a reusable bag that's strong enough for groceries and pretty enough for the beach. It has a solid oval base, a stretchy mesh body and comfortable handles.

Everything is worked in the round, from the bottom up, so there's no sewing – just crochet!

## Why You'll Love This Crochet Tote Bag

- **Eco-friendly** – say goodbye to plastic bags.
- **Stretches to hold a lot** but folds up small.
- **Made in one piece** with no sewing.
- **Uses basic stitches**: chain, single crochet and double crochet.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)

- **ch** – chain
- **sl st** – slip stitch
- **sc** – single crochet: insert hook, yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over, pull through both loops.
- **dc** – double crochet
- **inc** – increase: work 2 sc into the same stitch.
- **ch-1 sp** – the space under a ch 1.

## Size and Gauge

- **Finished size:** about 34 cm wide × 32 cm tall, not including handles.
- **Gauge:** 16 sc = 10 cm.

Cotton yarn is best for bags because it's strong and doesn't stretch out of shape.

## Step 1: Crochet the Oval Base

Ch 31.

**Round 1:** Sc in the 2nd ch from the hook, sc in the next 28 ch, work 3 sc in the last ch. Now turn your work so you're crocheting along the other side of the chain: sc in the next 28 ch, then 2 sc in the last ch (the same chain where you made your very first sc). Join with a sl st. **(62 sc)**

**Round 2:** Ch 1, inc in the first stitch, sc 28, inc in each of the next 3 stitches, sc 28, inc in each of the last 2 stitches. Join. **(68 sc)**

**Rounds 3–6:** Continue in sc, adding **6 increases** each round around the two curved ends and working 1 sc in every other stitch. **(98 sc at the end of Round 6)**

> **Why increase?** Adding stitches at the ends makes the oval grow bigger while staying flat. If your base starts to ruffle, you're increasing too much; if it cups, add one more increase.

## Step 2: Crochet the Mesh Body

**Round 7:** Ch 4 (this counts as 1 dc + ch 1). *Skip 1 stitch, dc in the next stitch, ch 1.* Repeat from * around, skipping the last stitch. Join to the 3rd ch of the starting ch-4. **(49 mesh spaces)**

**Round 8:** Sl st into the first ch-1 space. Ch 4, *dc in the next ch-1 space, ch 1.* Repeat around. Join.

Repeat Round 8 until the mesh measures about **26 cm** – roughly 18 rounds.

## Step 3: Top Band and Handles

**Rounds 1–4 of the band:** Ch 1, sc in each dc and each ch-1 space around. Join. **(98 sc)**

**Round 5 (handle openings):** Ch 1, sc 18, **ch 50, skip 12 stitches**, sc 37, **ch 50, skip 12 stitches**, sc 19. Join.

**Rounds 6–8:** Ch 1, sc in each stitch around, working 1 sc into each chain of the handles. Join.

Fasten off and weave in all ends.

## Beginner Tips

- **Mark the first stitch** of each base round with a stitch marker.
- **Count after every base round.** The stitch counts tell you everything is on track.
- **Try a shorter handle chain** (ch 35) if you prefer a hand-held bag instead of a shoulder bag.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

- **The base is curling up like a bowl.** Add one or two extra increases at the curved ends.
- **The handles are uneven.** Count the stitches between the handle openings – both sides must match.
- **The mesh leans to one side.** That's normal for work in the round; blocking with water helps straighten it.

## FAQ

### What is the best yarn for a crochet tote bag?

100% cotton or a cotton-linen blend. They're strong, don't stretch much and are easy to wash.

### Can a crochet bag hold heavy groceries?

Yes. Cotton mesh is surprisingly strong. For very heavy items, work the handles with a double strand of yarn.

### How do I make the bag bigger?

Start with a longer chain (add stitches in pairs), and work more mesh rounds.
