This crochet basket with lid pattern makes a stylish and functional accessory for shelf storage. You'll enjoy creating this textured bag that not only looks great but is also quite sturdy. The combination of basic stitches makes it easy to follow along, even if you're just starting out.

This pattern is perfect for beginners looking to enhance their skills. As we work through the steps together, you'll learn how to create a lovely basket that can be customized to fit into your home's decor.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern uses basic crochet stitches, making it accessible for those who are just starting out. You'll reinforce your understanding of double crochet and single crochet while creating something functional.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- DC – double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- RT – round turn: a technique used when starting a round to create a clean edge and avoid gaps.

## Gauge and Finished Size
- Gauge: 12 stitches and 14 rows in double crochet (DC) = 10 cm (4 in).
- Finished dimensions: 
  - Basket: 25 cm × 25 cm (10 in × 10 in)
  - Lid: 25 cm (10 in) in diameter.

To ensure a proper fit, checking your gauge is important. If your swatch doesn't match, you may need to adjust your hook size.

## Step 1: Making the Basket Body
The basket body is worked in rows.

1. **Starting Chain**: Ch 50. 
2. **Row 1**: DC in the 4th ch from hook, DC in each ch across. (48 DC)
3. **Row 2**: Ch 3 (counts as first DC), turn, DC in the next DC and in each DC across. (48 DC)
4. **Rows 3–10**: Repeat Row 2 for a total of 9 more rows.
5. **Row 11**: Ch 1, turn, working in back loops only (BLO), SC in each DC across. (48 SC)
6. **Row 12**: Ch 1, turn, SC in the first SC and in each SC across. (48 SC)
7. **Row 13**: Ch 2, turn, DC in the first SC and in each SC across. (48 DC)
8. **Rows 14–22**: Repeat Row 7 until you have a total of 10 rows of DC.
9. Fold the panel into a basket shape and slip stitch the sides together, ensuring the bottom stays flat.

## Step 2: Crochet the Basket Lid
The basket lid is made similarly to the body.

1. **Starting Chain**: Ch 50. 
2. **Row 1**: DC in the 4th ch from hook, DC in each ch across. (48 DC)
3. **Row 2**: Ch 3, turn, DC in the next DC and in each DC across. (48 DC)
4. **Rows 3–5**: Repeat Row 2 for a total of 3 more rows.
5. **Row 6**: Ch 1, turn, SC in the first DC and in each DC across. (48 SC)
6. **Row 7**: Ch 1, turn, SC in the first SC and in each SC across. (48 SC)
7. **Row 8**: Ch 2, turn, DC in the first SC and in each SC across. (48 DC)

## Step 3: Finishing the Lid
1. Fold the lid and seam the two sides together with slip stitches.
2. Attach the button to the center of the basket's front. Create a loop on the lid by chaining 10, join to the first chain with a slip stitch to form a loop.

## How to Join Your Pieces
Weave in all ends with a tapestry needle. Block the basket and lid to help shape and flatten them. Make sure the lid fits securely over the basket.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Basket
- Keep your workspace tidy so you can easily find your materials.
- Maintain consistent tension in your stitches for an even finish.
- Regularly check the dimensions to ensure your basket will hold its shape.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My basket is uneven." – This could be due to inconsistent tension. Make sure you’re crocheting with a steady gauge throughout.
- "The lid doesn’t fit." – If the lid is too small, you may have crocheted too tightly. Check your gauge and adjust if necessary.
- "My stitches look loose." – This can happen if you're unraveling and redoing parts. Tighten your grip as you crochet or try a smaller hook.

## How to Care for Your Basket
For care, hand wash in cold water to maintain shape and color. Lay flat to dry completely before using to ensure it keeps its nice form.

### FAQ
### Can I customize the size?
Absolutely! You can adjust the starting chain to make a larger or smaller basket. Just remember to keep the stitch counts consistent to maintain the shape.

### What if I don’t have cotton yarn?
You can use any worsted weight yarn, but be mindful that the texture and durability may differ from cotton.

### How long will this take to finish?
The estimated total time for making the basket and lid is about 3 hours.
