This crochet kids cardigan pattern makes a stylish and cozy cardigan for children, featuring fold-over mitten cuffs to keep little hands snug. It's a wonderful way to practice your double crochet stitches while creating an adorable piece that’s perfect for layering in cooler weather.

This pattern suits beginners ready for a slightly more challenging project. You'll learn how to create armholes and cute mitt cuffs while working with simple techniques. Let’s get started on The Mitt Cardi together!

## Why You'll Love This Kids Cardigan
- Cozy and functional – perfect for keeping your child warm during chilly days.
- Fold-over mitten cuffs – offer extra warmth and are a fun feature!
- Easy to customize – choose your favorite colors to make it unique.
- Suitable for a range of sizes – the pattern fits kids aged 4 to 6 years.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern uses basic stitches like double crochet, making it beginner-friendly. You'll also learn how to create armholes and work in the back loops for the fold-over cuffs. Don't worry, you'll get the hang of it!

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- st(s) – stitch(es)

**Special Stitch - Fold-Over Mitten Cuff:**
- Fold-Over Mitten Cuff: Work in back loops only to create a flexible edge, enabling the cuff to fold over.

## Size and Gauge
- Finished sizes: Fits children aged 4 years (chest 61 cm / 24 in), 5 years (chest 66 cm / 26 in), 6 years (chest 71 cm / 28 in).
- Length: 46 cm (18 in)
- Sleeve length (from shoulder): 30.5 cm (12 in)
- Gauge: 12 dc and 7 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in double crochet.

To check gauge, make a swatch with 20 dc and 14 rows, then measure. Adjust your hook size as needed to match the gauge.

## Step 1: Crochet the Body
The body is worked in rows. 
1. **Foundation Chain:** 
   - Ch 78 (4 years), 82 (5 years), or 86 (6 years).
2. **Row 1:** 
   - Dc in the fourth ch from hook (counts as 2 dc). Dc in next 74 (78, 82) ch. Turn. (76 sts for all sizes)
3. **Row 2:** 
   - Ch 3 (counts as first dc). Dc in each st across. Turn. (76 sts)
4. **Rows 3-14:** 
   - Repeat Row 2 for a total of 13 rows (including Row 1).
5. **Row 15 (armhole separation):**
   - Ch 3, dc in next 32 (34, 36) sts. Ch 10 (10, 10) for the armhole. Skip 10 (10, 10) sts, dc in next 32 (34, 36) sts. Turn. (64 sts)
6. **Row 16:** 
   - Ch 3, dc in each st across to the armhole chain.
7. **Row 17:** 
   - Jumping to the armhole chain: Ch 3, turn, dc in each chain across (10 dc). 
8. **Row 18:** 
   - Skip over to the main body, dc in next 32 (34, 36) sts across to end. Turn. (64 sts)
9. **Rows 19-26:** 
   - Repeat Rows 16-18 for a total of 8 rows. (64 sts)
10. **Row 27:** 
   - Ch 1, sc around each edge (or slip stitch if preferred) to finish off; sl st in last stitch, fasten off.

## Step 2: Crochet the Sleeves
The sleeves are worked separately.
1. **Left Sleeve:** 
   - With MC and 5 mm hook, ch 34. 
2. **Row 1:** 
   - Dc in fourth ch from hook and in each ch across. Turn. (32 sts)
3. **Rows 2-11:** 
   - Ch 3, dc in each st across. (32 sts)
4. **Row 12:** 
   - Fold sleeve cuff (work in back loops) using dc in back loops only to create the fold-over mitten cuff. Fasten off. Repeat for the right sleeve.

## Step 3: Assembly & Finishing
1. **Joining the Body and Sleeves:** 
   - Attach sleeves to armholes using sl st or whip stitch.
2. **Edging:**
   - Work a round of sc around the entire cardigan edge, including the neckline and bottom hem.
3. **Buttons (optional):** 
   - Sew buttons on the front for closure.
4. **Weaving in Ends:** 
   - Weave in any remaining ends and block the cardigan to shape.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Cardigan
- Keep your yarn organized – it helps avoid tangles.
- Check your gauge before starting to ensure the right fit for your child.
- Maintain even tension as you crochet for a polished look.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- My cardigan is too small. – This may be due to not checking gauge. Always measure your swatch, and adjust your hook size if necessary.
- The stitches are uneven. – This might happen if your tension varies. Aim to keep a consistent grip on your yarn.
- My cuffs are too tight. – If they’re not folding over easily, consider chaining more for the sleeve or adding more rows before folding.

## How to Care for Your Cardigan
For best results, machine wash on a gentle cycle in cold water. Lay the cardigan flat to dry to maintain its shape and softness.

### What if I want to use a different yarn?
You can substitute different weight yarns, but make sure to check your gauge to ensure the finished cardigan fits your child properly.

The estimated total time for making this cardigan is around 8 to 11 hours, depending on your skill level and pace.
