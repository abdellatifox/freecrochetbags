This crochet throw blanket pattern makes a cozy and stylish addition to your living room. The Anchor Throw is specially designed to fit snugly on your sofa with unique corner grips that keep it in place. You'll be using simple stitches like double crochet, making this an enjoyable project. 

This pattern is great for intermediate crocheters looking to enhance their skills and learn how to create innovative design elements like the corner grips.

## Why You'll Love This Throw Blanket
- Thoughtfully designed – the corner grips ensure the blanket stays in place.
- Versatile colors – choose your favorite shades to match your decor.
- Cozy and functional – perfect for snuggling up on the couch!
- Great learning opportunity – practice your double crochet and gauge skills.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! While it's designated for intermediate crocheters, you'll be working with basic stitches and can take it step-by-step. Don't worry if you're unsure; just follow along, and you'll master the skills needed.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- sp – space
- inc – increase (2 dc in same stitch)
- dec – decrease

**Corner Grip:** Worked in rows, this creates a pocket that holds the throw on the sofa. To make it, you will use increases as you build height.

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** Approximately 127 × 152 cm (50 × 60 in).
- **Gauge:** 14 dc and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in double crochet.
- To ensure a perfect fit, check your gauge with a swatch. If it's too tight or loose, adjust your hook size.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Body of the Blanket
The body of the blanket is made up of simple double crochet stitches.

1. **Foundation Row:**
   - Ch 200.
   - Row 1: dc in 4th ch from hook, dc in each ch across. (198 dc)
2. **Row 2:**
   - Ch 3 (counts as first dc), turn. dc in each dc across. (198 dc)
3. **Rows 3–42:**
   - Repeat Row 2. You will have a total of 42 rows of dc stitches.

### Corner Grips (make 4)
These grip pockets will hold your blanket in place.

1. **Grip Row 1:**
   - Ch 2, 1 dc into the same stitch at each corner. (1 dc)
2. **Grip Row 2:**
   - Ch 2, 1 inc in each dc (twice), turn. (2 dc)
3. **Grip Rows 3–9:**
   - Repeat until grip is 9 rows tall.

Repeat these grip instructions for all four corners, adjusting placement on the throw as needed.

## How to Join Your Pieces
1. **Attaching Grips:**
   - Position each grip pocket on the corners of the throw and pin in place.
   - Using a tapestry needle and matching yarn, sew the edges of the grip to the throw.
2. **Adding Buttons:**
   - Sew a button approximately 1 inch below the top of each grip for a decorative touch.
3. **Weaving In Ends:**
   - Weave in all loose ends securely using a tapestry needle.
4. **Blocking:**
   - Lightly block the blanket to ensure straight edges and even corners.

## Beginner Tips
- Keep your workspace organized to avoid losing your tools.
- Focus on maintaining an even tension for a uniform finish.
- Check the size of your blanket as you go along to ensure it meets the desired dimensions.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My blanket is too small." – This could be due to a tight gauge; make sure to loosen your stitches.
- "The stitches look uneven." – This may happen if your tension varies; practice keeping it consistent.
- "My grips are sagging." – Ensure you’re attaching them securely and that your corner grips are tall enough.

## How to Care for Your Throw Blanket
Hand wash your throw blanket in cold water with mild soap and let it lay flat to dry. Avoid wringing to maintain its shape and quality.
