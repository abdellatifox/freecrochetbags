This crochet baby blanket with pacifier pocket (The Paci Corner) features a cozy design that’s practical and adorable. The added pacifier pocket keeps pacifiers clean and handy, making it ideal for newborns and infants. With simple stitches like single crochet and double crochet, this pattern is easy to follow and enjoyable to make.

You'll learn to create a lovely baby blanket while also mastering how to add a functional pocket. It's a wonderful project for new crocheters looking to make something useful for little ones.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern uses basic stitches and simple construction. You'll be guided through each step clearly, making it easy to grasp, even for new crocheters.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sc – single crochet
- dc – double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- inc – increase (2 sc in the same st)
- dec – decrease (single crochet two stitches together)

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** Approximately 76 × 91 cm (30 × 36 in)
- **Gauge:** 12 sc and 14 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in single crochet. To check gauge, make a swatch of 16 stitches and 16 rows, then measure after washing and blocking.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Blanket Body
The blanket body is worked in rows.

1. **Foundation Row:** Ch 132.
2. **Row 1:** Sc in 2nd ch from hook, sc across. (131 sts)
3. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (131 sts)
4. **Repeat Row 2** until the blanket measures 91 cm (36 in) in length, ending with a total of 36 rows.
5. **Change to Accent Color:** Switch to Soft White.
6. **Row 37:** Ch 1, sc in each st across. (131 sts)
7. **Repeat Row 37** for 3 additional rows (total of 4 rows in Soft White).
8. **Fasten off** and weave in ends.

### Pacifier Pocket
The pocket is worked separately and sewn to the blanket.

1. **Foundation:** With Baby Blue, ch 26.
2. **Row 1:** Sc in 2nd ch from hook, sc across. (25 sts)
3. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (25 sts)
4. **Repeat Row 2** until the pocket measures 13 cm (5 in) in height (approximately 5 rows).
5. **Form Pocket:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in first 7 sts, ch 4, skip 4 sts, sc in last 7 sts. (25 sts)
6. **Row 6:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in first 7 sts, sc in each of the 4 chs (counting as one sc for assembly), sc in last 7 sts. (25 sts)
7. **Fasten off** and weave in ends.

## How to Attach the Pocket
1. Position the pocket on the desired location approximately 15 cm (6 in) from the top of the blanket.
2. Using the tapestry needle, sew the pocket's edges to the blanket, ensuring not to sew the top edge closed.

## Adding the Edging
1. With Soft White, make a sl st around the entire edge of the blanket.
2. In each corner, make 3 dc to create a corner.

## Finishing Tip: Weave Ends Neatly
Use the tapestry needle to weave all yarn ends securely. This will help give your blanket a tidy finish.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Baby Blanket
- Keep a consistent tension – it helps maintain even stitches.
- Check your gauge – measuring helps ensure the right fit and size.
- Organize your materials – having everything on hand makes the process smoother.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My blanket is too small." – You may not have made enough rows. Just continue until you reach the desired length.
- "The stitches are uneven." – This could be due to varying tension. Try to relax and keep your grip consistent.
- "The pocket is not attaching properly." – Ensure you sew the edges securely, leaving the top open for a pocket.

## How to Care for Your Baby Blanket
To keep your blanket soft and in good condition, hand wash it with mild detergent. Lay it flat to dry and avoid wringing out, which can distort its shape.

### FAQ
### Can I customize the size?
Yes! You can adjust the foundation chain to create a larger or smaller blanket. Just maintain the same stitch counts for the rows.

### What yarn can I use as a substitute?
Worsted weight (#4) acrylic yarn works best for this pattern. You can choose colors that suit your style while ensuring the weight matches.

### How long will it take to complete?
The total time is approximately 8–11 hours, depending on your skill level and how quickly you work.
