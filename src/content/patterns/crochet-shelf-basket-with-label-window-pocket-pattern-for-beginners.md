This crochet shelf basket pattern makes a beautifully functional addition to your home. Its stylish design features a unique label window pocket, perfect for organizing and identifying your items. We’ll be using easy stitches like single crochet and half double crochet, so it's beginner-friendly and enjoyable to make.

This pattern is ideal for those new to crochet or anyone looking to create a practical, stylish storage solution for their home. By working through this project, you’ll gain confidence in your crochet skills while creating something truly useful.

## Why You'll Love This Crochet Shelf Basket
- Functional design – great for organizing your shelf space.
- Unique label pocket – easily identify what's inside.
- Fast to make – complete in about 5–7 hours!
- Simple stitches – perfect for beginner crocheters.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern uses basic stitches like single crochet and half double crochet. You’ll learn how to create a pocket and work in rounds, building skills as you go.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sc – single crochet
- hdc – half double crochet
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- sl st – slip stitch
- rnd – round

## Size and Gauge
- Finished Size: Approximately 20 × 25 × 15 cm (8 × 10 × 6 in)
- Gauge: 11 sts and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in single crochet.

To check your gauge, make a 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm) swatch in single crochet. If your gauge doesn't match, adjust your hook size or yarn.

## Step 1: Making the Basket Body
The basket body is worked in rows to create a solid structure.
1. Foundation Row: Ch 36. (36 sts)
2. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, sc across to end. (35 sts)
3. Rows 2–8: Ch 1, sc in each st across, turn. (35 sts)
4. Row 9: Ch 1, (hdc in each of the first 5 sts, hdc increase in next st) repeat across to last 5 sts, hdc in each of the last 5 sts. (42 sts)
5. Rows 10–20: Ch 1, sc in each st across, turn. (42 sts)

## Step 2: Adding the Label Window Pocket
We'll create a useful pocket using a contrast color.
1. Row 1 (CC): With contrast color, ch 12, sc in the 2nd ch from hook and each ch across. (11 sts)
2. Rows 2–4: Ch 1, sc across. (11 sts)
3. Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing.

## Step 3: Attaching the Pocket
We will attach the pocket to the basket next.
1. With MC, sew the pocket onto the basket body using a tapestry needle, centering it about 5 cm (2 in) from the bottom.
2. Sew the piece of clear plastic inside the pocket.

## Step 4: Finishing the Top Edge
Now, we’ll finish the top edge by working in rounds.
1. Row 1: (Working in the round now) join yarn at any st, ch 1, sc in each st around, join with a sl st. (42 sts)
2. Row 2: Ch 1, (sc in next 3 sts, sc increase in next st) repeat around. (52 sts)
3. Rows 3–4: Ch 1, sc in each st around, join with a sl st. (52 sts)
4. Fasten off and weave in ends.

## How to Line Your Basket (Optional)
If you're using fabric for lining:
- Pin the fabric inside the basket, aligning with the top edge.
- Hand stitch it in place for a neat finish.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Basket
- Keep your yarn organized to avoid tangles.
- Check your tension regularly to maintain consistent stitch sizes.
- Measure as you go to ensure your project stays the right shape.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My basket is uneven." – This might happen from skipping stitches. Count your stitches regularly as you go.
- "The pocket doesn't line up." – Double-check the placement before you sew it on.
- "My gauge is off." – If your swatch doesn't match the gauge, try a different hook size.

## How to Care for Your Crochet Shelf Basket
To keep your basket looking its best, hand wash with mild soap and lay it flat to dry. Avoid high heat to maintain its shape.

## FAQ
### Can I customize the size?
Absolutely! To make a larger basket, simply increase the foundation chain by increments of 6.

### What if I don't have cotton yarn?
You can substitute with another worsted weight yarn, but be mindful of the texture and strength required for a sturdy basket.

### How long will this take to make?
Expect to spend about 5–7 hours total, depending on your skill level and speed.
