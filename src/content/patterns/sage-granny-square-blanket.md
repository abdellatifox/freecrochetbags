If you've ever wanted to make a blanket but felt it was too big a project, this **easy granny square blanket crochet pattern** is the perfect place to start. You make one small square at a time, so every evening ends with something finished. Once you have enough squares, you simply join them together.

This free pattern uses only **three basic stitches**, and every step is explained in plain language.

## Why You'll Love This Granny Square Blanket

- **Small, portable pieces** – each square fits in your bag.
- **Only basic stitches** – chain, slip stitch and double crochet.
- **Easy to resize** – add or remove squares to make a baby blanket or a bed throw.
- **Calm colors** – sage and cream look lovely in any room.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?

Yes! If you can make a chain and a double crochet, you can make this blanket. The pattern repeats the same few steps over and over, which makes it a wonderful way to build confidence. New to double crochet? Practice a few rows on a spare chain first.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)

- **ch** – chain: yarn over and pull through the loop on your hook.
- **sl st** – slip stitch: insert hook, yarn over, pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook.
- **dc** – double crochet: yarn over, insert hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (3 loops on hook), yarn over and pull through 2 loops, yarn over and pull through the last 2 loops.
- **sp** – space: the gap under a chain, where you'll place your stitches.
- **corner** – the ch-2 space at each corner of the square.

## Gauge and Finished Size

- One finished square: **15 × 15 cm (6 × 6 in)** after 6 rounds.
- Finished blanket: **75 × 105 cm (30 × 42 in)** – 35 squares, 5 across and 7 down.

Gauge simply means "how big your stitches are". If your first square is much smaller, try a bigger hook; if it's bigger, try a smaller hook.

## How to Crochet a Granny Square (Step by Step)

### Round 1

With sage, ch 4 and join with a sl st into the first chain to make a small ring.

Ch 3 (this counts as your first dc). Work 2 dc into the center of the ring. *Ch 2, work 3 dc into the ring.* Repeat from * two more times. Ch 2 and join with a sl st to the top of the first ch-3.

**You now have:** 4 groups of 3 dc and 4 corners.

### Round 2

Sl st across the next 2 dc and into the corner space so you're in the right spot to start.

Ch 3, then work (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) all in the same corner. Ch 1. *Work (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in the next corner, ch 1.* Repeat from * two more times and join to the top of the ch-3.

**You now have:** 8 groups of 3 dc (2 on each side).

### Round 3

Fasten off sage and join cream in any corner.

Ch 3, (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in that corner. *Ch 1, 3 dc in the next ch-1 space along the side, ch 1, (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in the next corner.* Repeat around, ending with ch 1, 3 dc in the last side space, ch 1, and join.

### Rounds 4–6

Keep working the same way: **(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in every corner** and **3 dc in every ch-1 space along the sides**, with ch 1 between groups. Each round adds one more group of 3 dc on every side.

Use this color order: Round 4 sage, Round 5 cream, Round 6 sage.

> **Beginner tip:** weave in your yarn ends after every square. It takes one minute now and saves hours at the end!

Make **35 squares** in total.

## How to Join Your Granny Squares

1. Lay your squares out on a bed or the floor, 5 across and 7 down.
2. Hold two squares with the right sides facing up, edges side by side.
3. With cream, sl st through the **back loops only** of both squares, stitch by stitch, along the edge.
4. Join all the rows first, then all the columns.

Joining through the back loops creates a neat, flat ridge that looks like a little frame around each square.

## Adding the Border

Join sage in any corner of the blanket. Work **2 rounds of dc** all the way around, placing (2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in each of the 4 corners and 1 dc in every stitch and space along the sides. Fasten off and weave in the ends.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Blanket

- **Count your groups** at the end of every round – it catches mistakes early.
- **Keep your tension relaxed.** Tight stitches make squares curl.
- **Block your squares** by pinning them flat, spraying with water and letting them dry. They'll be the same size and much easier to join.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

- **My square is twisting.** You probably missed a ch-1 between groups. Count the spaces on each side.
- **My square looks like a circle.** Check that you have exactly ch 2 in each corner, not ch 1.
- **My squares are different sizes.** This usually comes from tension changing. Blocking fixes small differences.

## How to Care for Your Blanket

Machine wash cold on a gentle cycle and dry flat. Cotton-blend yarn gets softer with every wash.

## FAQ

### How many granny squares do I need for a blanket?

For this 75 × 105 cm throw you need 35 squares. For a baby blanket, 25 squares (5 × 5) works well. For a single bed, try 7 × 10 squares.

### Can I use a different yarn?

Yes. Any worsted (weight 4) yarn works. Just check that your first square is close to 15 cm, and adjust your hook size if needed.

### How long does it take to make one granny square?

Most beginners take 20–30 minutes per square. You'll get faster after the first few!
