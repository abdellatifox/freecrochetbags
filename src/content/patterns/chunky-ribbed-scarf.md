Looking for your very first project? This **easy crochet scarf pattern for beginners** uses just **one stitch**, the half double crochet, and chunky yarn that works up fast. By working into the back loop only, you get a soft, stretchy ribbed texture that looks much fancier than it is.

## Why This Is the Perfect First Scarf

- **One stitch only** – once you learn it, you just repeat.
- **Chunky yarn** – you'll see progress quickly and can finish in a weekend.
- **Straight edges** – the pattern is easy to count and check.
- **Unisex and timeless** – make one for everyone you love.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)

- **ch** – chain
- **hdc** – half double crochet: yarn over, insert hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (3 loops on hook), yarn over and pull through all 3 loops.
- **BLO** – back loop only: every stitch has two loops on top. Insert your hook only under the one farthest from you.

## Size and Gauge

- **Finished size:** about 22 × 180 cm (9 × 71 in).
- **Gauge:** 9 hdc and 7 rows = 10 cm.

Gauge isn't very important for a scarf – if yours is a little wider or narrower, that's perfectly fine!

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Foundation

Ch 22.

### Row 1

Hdc in the 3rd ch from the hook (the 2 skipped chains help you reach the right height). Hdc in each ch across. Turn. **(20 hdc)**

### Row 2

Ch 1 (does not count as a stitch). Hdc in the **BLO** of each stitch across. Turn. **(20 hdc)**

### Repeat

Repeat Row 2 until your scarf is about **180 cm long** – roughly 125 rows. Try it on around your neck to find the length you like best.

> **Beginner tip:** count your stitches at the end of every few rows. If you have 19 or 21, you've missed or added a stitch at the edge – the first and last stitches are the easiest to miss.

## Finishing Your Scarf

Fasten off and use your tapestry needle to weave the yarn tails back and forth through the stitches.

**Optional fringe:** cut 40 pieces of yarn, each 30 cm long. Fold two pieces in half, pull the fold through a stitch at the short end with your hook, then pull the tails through the loop. Repeat across both ends.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

- **My scarf is getting wider.** You're probably working 2 stitches into the first or last stitch. Place a stitch marker in the last stitch of each row.
- **My scarf is getting narrower.** You're likely skipping the last stitch – it's often hidden. Look closely at the end of the row.
- **No ribs are showing.** Check that you're using the back loop, not the front loop.

## How to Care for Your Scarf

Follow the care instructions on your yarn label. Most acrylic yarns can be machine washed on a gentle, cool cycle and laid flat to dry.

## FAQ

### How long does it take a beginner to crochet a scarf?

With chunky yarn, most beginners finish this scarf in about 5–6 hours. That's a relaxing weekend project.

### How much yarn do I need for a crochet scarf?

About 350 m of bulky yarn for this 180 cm scarf. For a shorter kids' scarf (120 cm), you'll need about 240 m.

### Can I use a different stitch?

Yes – try single crochet for a denser scarf, or double crochet for a lighter, faster one. Keep working in the back loop for ribs.
